# Research scope with incomplete event coverage

`research.route_horizon` includes `research_capability_scope`. Use this alongside the routed dates and event coverage when deciding which requested work is feasible. It does not score the quality or completeness of the final answer.

A missing future calendar does not prevent describing available events, reporting unknown dates, or discussing qualitative gap and liquidity exposure over the requested horizon. Preserve that horizon. Keep unsupported issuer facts and quantified risks unresolved. The router cannot establish a terminal forecast, valuation, ranking, or position size from calendar coverage.

An optional `event_exclusion_requirement` declares `not_declared` (default), `none`, `entry_only`, or `full_hold`. Set it from the actual request, not an analyst preference. An explicit exclusion requirement remains uncertified until separately evaluated using the relevant event evidence and policy. Even complete declared calendar coverage does not prove an event-free hold. This field records scope; it never creates a blackout or trade restriction itself. Its omission means not declared to this router, not permission to ignore a separately supplied event policy; the router does not evaluate other policies, including any policy inside `fixed_spec`.

For flexible reviews, describe known dates and later unknowns, then propose a refresh cadence only if useful. No cadence or issuer date is generated by this output, and no automation is activated. Resolve any `horizon_conflicts` before drawing horizon-dependent conclusions; descriptive observations can still be reported with that limitation.

Example: “Review this industrial issuer for four years with a flexible exit, permitting holdings through earnings. Use announced events, distinguish later unknown dates, and explain qualitative risks. Show numerical sensitivities only where inputs support them.” Partial coverage should yield feasible descriptive work and explicit uncertified claims, rather than a substituted short-term horizon or a requirement for four years of announced earnings dates.
